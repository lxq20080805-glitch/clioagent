-Goal-
Extract a small, source-grounded knowledge graph from IB History textbooks and
academic papers. Connect historical evidence to historiographical interpretations
without inventing authorship, disagreement, quotations, or facts.

-Entity types-
Use only one of these types: [{entity_types}]

HISTORIAN: a scholar explicitly named in the text.
HISTORICAL_EVENT: a dated event, development, policy, decision, or process.
HISTORICAL_EVIDENCE: a primary-source statement, statistic, documented action,
law, treaty, record, or other concrete evidence used to support an interpretation.
INTERPRETATION: a distinct explanatory or evaluative historical claim explicitly
attributed to a historian or to the author of the current paper.

-Rules-
1. Separate evidence from interpretation.
2. Create an INTERPRETATION only when the text explicitly supports the attribution.
3. Distinguish the current author's position from historians whom they quote,
summarize, criticize, or cite.
4. Never infer a historian's position from general knowledge.
5. Do not create a disagreement merely because two interpretations differ.
6. Preserve author, title, year, and page markers in descriptions when the input
provides them. Never invent missing bibliographic details or page numbers.
7. Relationship descriptions must begin with exactly one label:
[ARGUES], [SUPPORTED_BY], [CHALLENGES], [QUALIFIES], or [RELATES_TO].
8. If attribution or evidence is ambiguous, describe the uncertainty explicitly.

-Steps-
1. Identify all qualifying entities. Format each as:
("entity"<|><entity_name><|><entity_type><|><entity_description>)

2. Identify only relationships explicitly supported by the input. Format each as:
("relationship"<|><source_entity><|><target_entity><|><relationship_description><|><relationship_strength>)

Use relationship strength 8-10 for direct, explicit statements; 5-7 for clear
paraphrases; and 1-4 for uncertain or indirect connections.

3. Return output in English as one list. Use ## as the list delimiter.
4. When finished, output <|COMPLETE|>.

-Example-
Entity_types: HISTORIAN,HISTORICAL_EVENT,HISTORICAL_EVIDENCE,INTERPRETATION
Text:
[PAGE 42] Historian Jane Smith argues that wartime shortages weakened support for
the government. She supports this interpretation with the 1917 bread ration data.

Output:
("entity"<|>JANE SMITH<|>HISTORIAN<|>Historian named as the author of an interpretation on page 42)
##
("entity"<|>WARTIME SHORTAGES WEAKENED GOVERNMENT SUPPORT<|>INTERPRETATION<|>Jane Smith's interpretation that wartime shortages weakened support for the government, stated on page 42)
##
("entity"<|>1917 BREAD RATION DATA<|>HISTORICAL_EVIDENCE<|>Bread ration data from 1917 used as evidence on page 42)
##
("relationship"<|>JANE SMITH<|>WARTIME SHORTAGES WEAKENED GOVERNMENT SUPPORT<|>[ARGUES] Jane Smith explicitly advances this interpretation on page 42<|>10)
##
("relationship"<|>WARTIME SHORTAGES WEAKENED GOVERNMENT SUPPORT<|>1917 BREAD RATION DATA<|>[SUPPORTED_BY] The interpretation explicitly uses the 1917 bread ration data on page 42<|>9)
<|COMPLETE|>
